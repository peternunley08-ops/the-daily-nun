Working identity for The Daily Nun, by Peter Nunley. This is an unpublished concept and draft editorial package.

## The idea

A personal, independent publication about the things Peter wants to understand: government, media, culture, and the questions in between.

**Masthead:** The Daily Nun  
**Byline:** Peter Nunley  
**Tagline:** A closer look at the world.  
**Suggested Instagram bio:** A closer look at government, media, culture & whatever has me curious. Written by Peter Nunley.

Keep the name broad. Let curiosity connect the subjects. Begin with one substantial piece each week, accompanied by one explanatory Instagram carousel and one shorter notebook post. “Daily” is the publication's name; the schedule can grow with Peter's interests and available time.

## Recommended format

- A newspaper-style website serves as the durable, readable home for complete articles and their sources.
- Instagram carousels explain one question at a time and invite readers into the complete article.
- Begin with text and strong typography; add narrated video when Peter wants that format.
- Treat downloadable newspaper issues as occasional collections after enough articles exist.

No publishing account, domain, or social handle has been created or checked. The local HTML is a visual proof, not a deployed website or an Instagram account.

## Editorial voice

Curious, specific, conversational, and willing to change its mind. Explain a concept before using its jargon. Keep reporting, interpretation, and personal opinion distinguishable in the writing. Link to original evidence near claims; name dates and denominators for statistics. An interesting question is a better starting point than a conclusion that needs defending.

## Design direction

Paper #f6f5f0; ink #20201c; blue #2448b5; secondary ink #68685f; rules #c8c8bc; white #ffffff. Georgia carries the masthead and editorial headlines; the system sans-serif handles navigation and metadata. Desktop resembles a newspaper front page; mobile uses a single readable column. The publication name leads the identity; use a plain Peter Nunley byline without nickname branding or a decorative signature.

Design review: newspaper rules and serif typography are intentional responses to the requested newspaper format. Blue annotations and a personal editor's note distinguish the identity. Avoid stock religious imagery, invented readership, fake social engagement, and decorative news charts without data.

## First Instagram carousel — draft

1. **Who decides what we see?** Your feed is where the question starts.
2. **Start with two questions.** Who made this story? Who put it in front of me?
3. **Those can be different companies.** A newsroom makes a report. A platform recommends it. A creator interprets it. Each makes choices along the way.
4. **A big number needs a source.** Before repeating “90% of the media,” ask: 90% of what, measured when, and by whom?
5. **Three questions to keep.** Who owns it? How did it reach me? What evidence can I inspect?
6. **Welcome to The Daily Nun.** Government. Media. Culture. Whatever makes us curious. A closer look at the world.
