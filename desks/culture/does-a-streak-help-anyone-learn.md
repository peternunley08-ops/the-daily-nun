# Does a streak help anyone learn?

*Streaks are good at bringing people back. Whether they help anyone learn more is a separate question, and The Daily Nun found no study that answers it.*

Picture this. It is 11:50 at night, and a phone lights up: the streak ends at midnight. A one-minute lesson saves it. Whether anything was learned in that minute, the counter does not say.

> A streak counts days, not what anyone learned.

## What the app says it is doing

Duolingo, a language-learning app built around daily streaks, has explained the design in its own words. In a January 2022 post, the company wrote that once the novelty of a streak wears off, "this is when we tap into 'loss aversion,'" which the post describes as a bias that makes people "particularly averse to losing something." The same post says that "losing your streak doesn't mean you lose the knowledge you've gained."

The company reports that the streak works on its own terms. In 2022 it said that letting learners hold two "streak freezes" raised the number of daily active learners by a relative 0.38%. In a November 2020 post, it said that making streaks easier to keep, by counting a single lesson a day, raised the number of learners on a 7-day streak by over 40%. These are the company's own figures from its own tests.

## What researchers found

Two studies point in different directions.

- **Habits survive a missed day.** In a 2009 study, 96 volunteers repeated an eating, drinking or exercise habit at the same point each day for 12 weeks. Missing one day "did not materially affect the habit formation process." The time it took a habit to reach its peak automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days.
- **Seeing a broken streak lowers engagement.** A 2023 set of seven studies found that people kept doing an activity more when a log showed them an unbroken streak than when it showed a broken one, even when their actual behavior was the same. The authors concluded that people treat keeping the streak as a goal in itself. Letting people "repair" a broken streak weakened the effect.

Put together: a missed day does little harm to a habit, but seeing the counter reset can lead people to do less.

## What is not known

None of these sources measures whether streaks help people learn more. Duolingo's figures measure how many people come back. The 2023 studies measure whether people keep doing an activity. Coming back is not the same as learning, and The Daily Nun found no independent study that tests the difference.

## The short version

- Duolingo says its streak uses "loss aversion" to keep people coming back.
- A missed day barely affects habit formation. A reset counter can still lead people to do less.
- No source here shows that streaks make anyone learn more.
