Proposed opening article

Who makes a story?
Who helps it find us?

I've been thinking about government, the news, and why people can come away from the same event with such different understandings of it. I don't want to start by assuming I already know the answer.

One question keeps opening into another. Who owns the outlet? Who chose the subject? Who did the reporting? Who decided this was the clip I should see? And what would I need to read to check the claim for myself?

Those questions belong together, but they ask about different things. Ownership tells us something about an institution. Reporting tells us how a story was established. Distribution tells us how it reached an audience. Understanding one doesn't automatically explain the others.

Take a hypothetical example: a local reporter examines a public contract. A newspaper publishes the findings. Someone makes a short video about them. A platform recommends that video to me. I may encounter the commentary before I ever see the documents or the reporter's work.

I want this first series to follow that route. I'll look at the companies involved, the choices people make, and the research on what different kinds of exposure can change. When a statistic sounds enormous, I want to know what it measures. When studies disagree, I want to understand what each one tested.

That is also the idea behind The Daily Nun. A question, some reading, a closer look, and writing that lets you follow the evidence with me.
