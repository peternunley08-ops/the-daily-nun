"When people get frustrated by a local decision, they usually tag the mayor. But in local government, authority is legally divided. Mayors propose budgets, but city councils vote on them. The Daily Nun."

The Daily NunCulture

Verified Source Ledger

  • Online Manipulation: Hidden Influences in a Digital World
    Daniel Susser, Beate Roessler, Helen Nissenbaum (Georgetown Law Technology Review, 4 Geo. L. Tech. Rev. 1) — January 2020 (volume dated 2019)
    Limits: One scholarly definition among several. The authors note that not everyone agrees hiddenness is required for manipulation. Definition passages read from the published PDF on 2026-11-13.
  • How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou
    TikTok (TikTok Newsroom) — June 18, 2020
    Limits: The company's own description from 2020. It is not independently audited and may not describe the current system.
  • Instagram Ranking Explained
    Adam Mosseri (Instagram) — May 31, 2023
    Limits: The company's own description from 2023. It lists signals and predictions but not their weights, and it is not independently audited.
  • On YouTube's recommendation system
    Cristos Goodrow (YouTube Official Blog) — September 15, 2021
    Limits: The company's own description from 2021. It says recommendations drive 'a significant amount' of viewership and gives no figure.
  • Engagement, user satisfaction, and the amplification of divisive content on social media
    Smitha Milli, Micah Carroll, Yike Wang, et al. (PNAS Nexus 4(3), pgaf062) — March 5, 2025
    Limits: 806 U.S. Twitter users recruited through a crowd-work platform, February 11-27, 2023. Sample skewed younger and more Democratic. One platform at one point in time; no long-term effects measured; only the first 10 tweets per timeline were surveyed.
  • How do social media feed algorithms affect attitudes and behavior in an election campaign?
    Andrew M. Guess, et al. (Science) — July 27, 2023
    Limits: Abstract only, verified through Europe PMC for the repository's media-democracy report (source E01); the publisher page returned HTTP 403 on 2026-11-13. Consenting Facebook and Instagram users over three months in 2020; not an estimate of all accumulated platform effects.
  • A Look Behind the Screens: Examining the Data Practices of Social Media and Video Streaming Services (press release)
    Federal Trade Commission staff (Federal Trade Commission) — September 19, 2024
    Limits: Based on orders issued in December 2020 to nine companies. Concerns data practices, not the effects of ranking on beliefs or purchases.
  • For shopping, phones are common and influencers have become a factor – especially for young adults
    Michelle Faverio and Monica Anderson (Pew Research Center) — November 21, 2022
    Limits: 6,034 U.S. adults, July 5-17, 2022. Denominator for the purchase figures is adult social media users. Self-reported; does not establish that posts caused purchases. No respondents under 18.
  • Psychological targeting as an effective approach to digital mass persuasion
    Sandra C. Matz, Michal Kosinski, Gideon Nave, David J. Stillwell (Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences) — November 13, 2017
    Limits: Abstract only, read through Europe PMC on 2026-11-13. Three experiments reaching over 3.5 million people. 'Up to' figures are best cases, not averages. Tests tailored ads, not feed ranking.
  • America's News Influencers
    Pew Research Center (Pew Research Center) — November 18, 2024
    Limits: 10,658 U.S. adults, July 15-August 4, 2024. Measures where people say they regularly get news, not whether it changed their views. No respondents under 18.
  • The small effects of political advertising are small regardless of context, message, sender, or receiver: Evidence from 59 real-time randomized experiments
    Alexander Coppock, Seth J. Hill, Lynn Vavreck (Science Advances) — September 2, 2020
    Limits: Abstract only, read through Europe PMC on 2026-11-13. 49 ads, 59 experiments, 34,000 people, 2016 U.S. presidential campaign. Survey experiments on ad exposure, not feed ranking.
  • A 2 million-person, campaign-wide field experiment shows how digital advertising affects voter turnout
    Minali Aggarwal, Jennifer Allen, Alexander Coppock, et al. (Nature Human Behaviour) — January 12, 2023
    Limits: Abstract only, read through Europe PMC on 2026-11-13. 2 million voters in five battleground states, 2020. Measures turnout, not trust. One partisan ad program.
  • The effects of Facebook and Instagram on the 2020 election: A deactivation experiment
    Hunt Allcott, Matthew Gentzkow, Winter Mason, et al. (Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences) — May 13, 2024
    Limits: Abstract only, read through Europe PMC on 2026-11-13. A randomized subset of 19,857 Facebook and 15,585 Instagram users deactivated for six weeks before the 2020 election. Tests access to the platforms, not the ranking system alone.
  • Out-group animosity drives engagement on social media
    Steve Rathje, Jay J. Van Bavel, Sander van der Linden (Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences) — June 2021
    Limits: Abstract only, read through Europe PMC on 2026-11-13 (also source M03 in the media-democracy report). 2,730,215 posts from news media and U.S. congressional accounts on Facebook and Twitter. Associations between wording and shares; does not show that posts made readers angrier or generated profit.

Voiceover Teleprompter

Read aloud while recording over vertical video asset (~15 sec).

"Every feed is a ranking. TikTok, Instagram and YouTube say their systems predict what each person will watch, like or share. In 2023, researchers audited the timelines of 806 Twitter users in the United States. Ranking by engagement showed them more angry, hostile posts than a simple newest-first feed, and they rated its political picks lower. One study is not the whole story. But clicking on something is not the same as wanting more of it. The Daily Nun."
