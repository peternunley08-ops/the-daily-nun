# The Daily Nun: source checks

Verified through gstack /browse on November 12, 2026. These are current media topics, not six claims of breaking news. The 2025 findings are visibly dated.

## Real face. Fake words.

[Disclosing use of GenAI content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)

Publisher: YouTube Help / Google. Source type: Current platform policy, not an independent audit.

YouTube requires disclosure of realistic AI content, including making a real person appear to say or do something they did not do.

Limits: The card describes a capability and disclosure policy. It does not claim all AI content is deceptive or that every misleading clip is labeled.

## Mad? That's the hook.

[The Oxford Word of the Year 2025 is rage bait](https://corp.oup.com/news/the-oxford-word-of-the-year-2025-is-rage-bait/)

Publisher: Oxford University Press / Oxford Languages. Published: December 1, 2025. Source type: Lexicographic definition and editorial selection.

Oxford defines rage bait as online content deliberately designed to provoke anger or outrage, typically to increase traffic or engagement.

Limits: This defines a tactic. It does not identify a particular post as intentional bait, prove an algorithm always rewards anger, or imply all anger is misplaced.

## Review or ad?

[Add branded content restrictions & disclosure labels](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235?hl=en)

Publisher: YouTube Help / Google. Source type: Current platform policy, not legal advice or an enforcement audit.

YouTube instructs creators featuring branded content, sponsorships, endorsements or other commercial relationships to select the paid-promotion setting, which adds a disclosure label.

Limits: The statement is limited to YouTube's rule. Sponsored content can be accurate; a missing label is not proof of a secret deal. It does not describe every platform's rules.

## Your feed is the front page.

[Social Media and News Fact Sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/fact-sheet/social-media-and-news-fact-sheet/)

Publisher: Pew Research Center. Published: October 19, 2026. Source type: Nationally representative survey / self-reported news habits.

The fact sheet's demographic table reports 43% for regular Instagram news consumption among U.S. adults ages 18–29.

Survey conducted August 24–30, 2026 among 5,166 U.S. adults overall. The 43% denominator is U.S. adults ages 18–29, not Instagram users and not all members of Gen Z. Platform users were asked whether they regularly get news there. The estimate does not measure news accuracy or exclusive reliance on Instagram.

Limits: The headline is an editorial metaphor. The survey does not establish that Instagram is this group's main or only news source. Sample size is for the full survey, not the age subgroup.

## Your fave is the news desk.

[News Influencers Fact Sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/fact-sheet/news-influencers-fact-sheet/)

Publisher: Pew Research Center / Pew-Knight Initiative. Published: November 4, 2025. Source type: Nationally representative survey / self-reported news habits.

Pew reports that 38% of U.S. adults ages 18–29 say they regularly get news from news influencers on social media.

Survey conducted August 18–24, 2025. Denominator: U.S. adults ages 18–29. News influencers were defined as individuals with a large social-media following who often post about news or political or social issues. Only respondents reporting social-media news use were asked the influencer question; Pew reports the estimate for the age population. This is not a new 2026 finding.

Limits: The headline is an editorial metaphor. These are self-reported habits, not proof that creators are more or less accurate than journalists. 'Ages 18–29' is not identical to the entire Gen Z population.

## Same app. Different feeds.

[How TikTok recommends content](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content)

Publisher: TikTok. Source type: Platform explanation, not an independent algorithm audit.

TikTok describes user interactions, content information, and user information as recommendation factors. It says interactions, including watch time, generally receive greater weight for most users and that feeds remain unique to each person.

Limits: The card attributes the explanation to TikTok. It does not establish that personalization causes polarization or that feeds contain only one viewpoint.
